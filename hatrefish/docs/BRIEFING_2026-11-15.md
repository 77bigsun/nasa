## 1. What we are building

We look for places on Earth that resemble specific places on Mars. "Resemble" means one property at a time: terrain, rock, temperature, dryness, ice, dust. For each Mars site we score every land cell on Earth, rank the cells, and say where the resemblance holds and where it breaks. We then ask which Earth sites a team would need to visit to rehearse every mission test.

## 2. How the system works

1. **Mars targets (7):** Jezero, Gale, Arcadia (from IanChen), plus four SkyFall reference sites: AP-105, Protonilus, Oxia, Meridiani. Each gets one value per property.
2. **Earth grid:** every land cell at 0.1° (about 11 km), about 1.44 million cells, built from public satellite data (MODIS, MERRA-2, ASTER, GLiM, ESA CCI permafrost, GMTED, and others).
3. **Per-property score (0–1):** the method depends on the property.
   - **Distance-based:** used when the two values can be compared directly, for example slope.
   - **Percentile-based:** used when Mars and Earth are measured differently, for example thermal inertia and dust.
   - **"More extreme is closer":** used when Mars is beyond anything on Earth, for example cold and dryness.
4. **Analog profile:** the properties are grouped into 5 scored dimensions: terrain, surface material, environment, water and ice, atmosphere and dust. Each dimension is the geometric mean of its properties. The overall score is the geometric mean of the dimensions, × 100, with equal weights.
   - The output gives a top 20 overall, a top 5 for each dimension and each property, and a "where it breaks" list for every site.
   - Biology and caves have no global data, so they are not scored.
5. **Mission tests R1–R6:** landing surface, visual navigation, shallow ground ice, hydrated minerals, glacial landforms, dust and weather. Each test has its own features and thresholds.
   - R2 cannot be scored, because there is no global data for it.
   - R3 and R5 apply only to Mars sites that show ice.
6. **Set covering:** finds the fewest Earth sites that cover every (Mars site, test) pair. A site qualifies for a pair if it scores at least 90% of the best site for that pair. The program also draws the curve "each extra site adds N".
7. **Zoom:** the top candidates are re-scored at about 1 km to check the coarse result.
8. **Ranking rules:**
   - Only cells with at least 80% of their data present are ranked.
   - Sites must be at least 150 km apart.
   - Known analog sites within 50 km are flagged; everything else counts as a new candidate.

The "mission version", which used learned weights for 4 mission types, was retired on 14 Nov. The code is kept but not run.

## 5. Validation

There is no official list of the correct answers, so we use four kinds of evidence. The script is `scripts\validate_profiles.py` and writes `outputs\validation\VALIDATION.md`.

**The numbers below come from a test run on the old data.** Run the script once on the new data and use those numbers instead.

1. **Recovering known analog sites, one dimension at a time.**
   - Each known analog is scored only on the dimension it is known for, for example mineralogy → surface material.
   - Median global rank: top 9.8%, against 33% when ranked by the overall score. Random would be 50%.
   - 53% of them land in the global top 10%.
2. **Negative controls** (rainforests, river deltas, farmland): none of the 56 checks reaches the global top 25%. Their scores are 30–80 points below the top 20.
3. **Robustness:**
   - Changing any one dimension's weight by ±50% keeps 12–20 of the top 20.
   - Two other changes barely move the top 20: averaging the dimensions arithmetically instead of geometrically, and raising the minimum score from 0.01 to 0.05.
   - Changing the data-coverage cut-off or the minimum spacing also barely moves it.
   - Ranking by the worst dimension alone changes it more.
   - Each top-10 site gets a "stability" percentage. In the test run, 14 of 20 sites stayed in the top 20 in at least 80% of the scenarios.
4. **Regional recovery:** the share of each top 20 within 300 km of a known analog is mixed (0–20%, where random would be 3%). This is weak evidence; leave it out of the talk.

**Suggested line for the talk:** "Known analogs rank in the global top 10% on the property they are known for; obviously non-Mars places never reach the top quarter; and the top sites survive ±50% changes to the weights."

## 7. Limits to state openly

- **No ground truth:** the evidence is indirect (section 5).
- **Resolution:** the grid is about 11 km, so this is regional screening, not landing-site selection. Only the top candidates are checked at 1 km.
- **What Earth cannot copy:** gravity, air pressure, radiation and air composition. These are listed for every site and not scored.
- **What we cannot measure:** biology, caves and lava tubes, crater shape, and visual navigation (R2). Mineralogy is coarse.
- **Inputs:** global temperature is a 2020–2024 average while the zoom uses 2015–2024. Monthly temperature is derived, so the seasonal parts of R5 and R6 are approximate.
- **Design choices:** the thresholds for R1–R6, the set-covering pass mark, and the mapping of known analogs to dimensions were set by us, not learned from data.
- **Mars-side values:** each Mars site uses a single value per property, with no uncertainty attached.
- **Logistics are ignored:** set covering does not consider travel, season, permits or cost, so Antarctic sites score well but are hard to reach.
